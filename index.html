Research & Thesis

Counting and Sampling Complexity of Total Matchings

Topics: Graph Theory, Approximate Counting, Markov Chain Monte Carlo, Hardcore Model, Computational Complexity

The computational complexities of counting independent sets and matchings in graphs have been extensively studied in both exact and approximate regimes; however, the problem of counting total matchings—a structure uniting constraints of both independent set and matching—remains largely unexplored. In this dissertation, we establish the hardness of exact counting for #TOTAL MATCHINGS in general graphs. We then investigate approximate counting in bounded-degree graphs using moment analysis and thermodynamic methods inspired by the study of the hardcore model. Based on analysis introduced by Sly, Weitz, Mossel, and Wormald, we derive the first moment rate function, the corresponding stationary point equations, and a local stability criterion for the symmetric solution. Numerical analysis indicates that the structure of the stationary point equations changes as the density of matching edges varies, suggesting a phase transition phenomenon. We also discuss the difficulties involved in converting this structural analysis into a rigorous approximation hardness reduction.
